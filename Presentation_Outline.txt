DAA MINI PROJECT PRESENTATION
Campus Navigation and Path-Finding System

Slide 1 — Title
Campus Navigation and Path-Finding System
Design and Analysis of Algorithms | Team names / roll numbers

Slide 2 — Problem Statement
Visitors and students may struggle to find campus buildings.
Goal: find a minimum-distance walking route between two locations.

Slide 3 — Objectives
Represent campus as a weighted graph; implement Dijkstra and A*;
show route and distance; compare expanded nodes and execution time.

Slide 4 — Graph Model
Vertices = campus locations.
Edges = walkways.
Weights = walking distances in metres.
The included graph uses illustrative sample data.

Slide 5 — Dijkstra’s Algorithm
Repeatedly select the closest unsettled vertex and relax its edges.
Typical time: O((V + E) log V) with adjacency list and binary heap.
Works with non-negative edge weights.

Slide 6 — A* Search
Uses f(n) = g(n) + h(n).
g(n) = distance from start; h(n) = estimated distance to goal.
Performance depends on heuristic; use an admissible heuristic for optimality.

Slide 7 — System Architecture
Location selection → graph search → predecessor reconstruction →
route visualization → distance and performance results.

Slide 8 — Demonstration
Show start/destination selectors, route highlight, route list,
total distance, nodes expanded, and execution time.

Slide 9 — Testing and Comparison
Run both algorithms on the same pairs.
Record actual distance, expanded nodes, and timing.
Do not present invented performance values.

Slide 10 — Limitations and Future Work
Sample map; no GPS or live closures.
Future: real campus survey, accessible routing, blocked paths, GPS.

Slide 11 — Conclusion
The project demonstrates graph modelling, shortest-path algorithms,
complexity analysis, implementation, and experimental comparison.

Slide 12 — References and Q&A
Dijkstra (1959); Hart, Nilsson & Raphael (1968); Python documentation.
